# Customer Planning — Everyday Shopper Experience

This folder defines the **Customer Portal** as a daily-use ecommerce experience: fast to browse, safe to buy, easy to track, simple to return, and personalized without feeling noisy.

**Model references:** Amazon-style personalization and saved flows, Flipkart-style simple mobile-first discovery, Shopify storefront clarity, and modern marketplace expectations around tracking, offers, wishlist, support, and returns.

## North Star

**Find the right product quickly → trust it → buy without friction → track clearly → get help or return without stress.**

For a frequent customer, the portal should remember useful context: saved addresses, wishlist, cart, recently viewed products, preferred categories, order history, coupons, and support history.

For a new customer, it should not feel heavy: browse first, account later, clear category paths, simple product cards, obvious checkout steps, and visible trust signals.

---

## Customer Principles

1. **Discovery is the product.** Search, category navigation, filters, and recommendations matter as much as the product page.
2. **Checkout must feel short.** Saved addresses, payment choices, coupon preview, and clear totals reduce drop-off.
3. **Trust must be visible.** Store name, delivery promise, return policy, reviews, payment safety, and invoice access should be easy to find.
4. **Order status must be calm.** Customers should always know what happened, what happens next, and where to get help.
5. **Support should start from context.** Help must open from orders, returns, payments, and product pages with the right reference attached.
6. **Personalization should help, not interrupt.** Use browsing and purchase history for useful shelves, reminders, and alerts, not aggressive popups.
